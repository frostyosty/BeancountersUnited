# Domain

The glossary and worked examples for acct. Accounting presentation decisions (signs, grouping, wording,
rounding placement) are recorded here once the practice has answered them. Nothing here is guessed. A
question that isn't answered yet is listed under "Open" with the milestone that needs it.

## Glossary

**Practice.** The accounting firm using acct. It owns the practice-level defaults: master charts, asset
classes, templates, mappings, the paragraph library and its own settings. Tax rates (GST and income tax)
aren't practice settings. They're versioned data in core, chosen by date.

**Client.** A business or entity the practice prepares accounts for. Each client has an entity type
(company or sole trader; others later).

**Tax to pay if submitted.** The figure on the owner's screen: GST, income tax and student loan on every
unfiled return with uploaded lines. It starts from conservative assumptions, so it's never lower than
what IRD would ask for on those statements.

**Save tax.** The queue that brings the figure down, one card at a time, biggest saving first. A card is a
question (such as "Is this business your only income?") or transactions to categorise.

**Not yet sorted.** The holding accounts that imported lines post to until someone categorises them: 140
for money in, which counts as income, and 965 for money out, which counts as drawings.

**Gap journal.** A system journal for a run of days that no statement covers. It posts the change in
balance across the gap, so the ledger still reconciles and a rise still counts as income.

**Client-year.** One client's financial year, with explicit start and end dates. It's the unit that holds
journals, gets finalised and produces statements. A first or last year can be shorter or longer than 12
months.

**Master chart.** The practice's chart of accounts for one entity type. A client's chart starts as a copy of
it and then belongs to the client.

**Chart / account.** A client's list of accounts. Each account has a code, a name, a type (asset,
liability, equity, income or expense) and an active flag.

**Account code.** An account's identifier, such as `200` or `200.01`. Codes sort by numeric segment, so
`200` < `200.01` < `1100`.

**Journal.** A dated set of at least two lines that sum to zero. Debits are positive and credits negative.
Posted journals never change.

**Reversing journal.** A journal that cancels an earlier one (`reverses_journal_id`). It's the only way to
correct a posted journal.

**System journal.** A journal the system generates, from TB import or depreciation. It's regenerated by
reversing the old one and posting a new one inside one command.

**Trial balance (TB).** Each account's balance at a date, computed from the opening balances plus the
year's journals.

**TB import.** Loading a client's trial balance from their own books. The TB-import journal is the
imported TB less the computed opening balances, so journals posted on top of it (adjustments) survive a
re-import.

**Books source.** Where a client-year's transactions come from: TB import or bank coding, never both.

**Rollover.** Computing a year's opening balances from the prior year. Balance-sheet accounts carry
forward. P&L accounts close to the client's designated equity account: retained earnings for a company,
owner's equity (970) for a sole trader, whose capital, drawings and "money out, not yet sorted" accounts
close to it as well. Rollover is computed, never posted.

**Template.** The layout of a set of statements for an entity type: a tree of report lines (groups, lines,
subtotals and totals), each with a stable key and a presentation sign.

**Report line.** One line in a template, identified by its key.

**Mapping.** A versioned set of account-code ranges, each pointing to a report line. Every account with a
non-zero balance must map to exactly one line.

**ReportDoc.** The built statements as data: sections, lines, current and prior amounts, styles and the
account codes behind each line. See ADR 003.

**Rounding line.** A designated report line that absorbs the differences left when individual lines are
rounded to whole dollars, so every column foots.

**Comparatives.** The prior year's figures shown beside the current year's. They come from the prior
year's snapshot TB presented through the current year's mapping and template.

**Finalise.** Lock a client-year once its statements are signed. It stores a snapshot and rejects any
later change to the year's numbers. Only a master user can reopen a year, and they must give a reason.

**Snapshot.** What finalising stores: the TB, the pinned mapping and template versions, the ReportDoc and
a SHA-256 of the ReportDoc JSON.

**Paragraph library.** The practice's own text for accounting policies and notes, with client fields
filled in.

**Asset register.** A client's fixed assets, with cost, acquisition date, depreciation settings and any
disposal. It drives depreciation journals and the fixed asset schedule.

**Asset class.** A practice-level grouping of assets with a default depreciation method, rate and
conventions, plus the accounts its journals post to. Clients can override these.

**Rate source.** Where an asset's depreciation settings came from: `practice`, `client` or `custom`.

**DV (diminishing value).** Depreciation at a rate on the book value at the start of the year (or on cost in
the year of acquisition).

**SL (straight line).** Depreciation at a rate on cost less residual value, stopping at the residual.

**Part-year convention.** How depreciation is prorated in the year an asset is acquired.

**Disposal-year convention.** How depreciation is treated in the year an asset is disposed of.

**GST-exclusive ledger.** A ledger whose coded bank lines are split into the net amount and GST.

## Decisions

### Rounding to whole dollars (decided 2026-09-23)
Statements show whole dollars. Rounding follows these rules, applied separately to the current and prior
columns:

1. **Lines.** Each line's exact cents total is rounded half away from zero.
2. **Totals.** Every total and subtotal shows its own exact cents total, rounded half away from zero. It
   is never just the sum of the rounded lines.
3. **Absorbing the difference.** Where the rounded lines in a section don't add up to that section's
   rounded total, the whole difference goes onto one line in that section, so the column foots. It's
   usually $1 but can be more when a section has many lines.
4. **Which line absorbs it.** Each client has a *rounding priority list*: an ordered list of account
   codes. For a section needing an adjustment, take the first account on the list that maps into that
   section and has a non-zero balance in that column. The line it maps to absorbs the difference.
   Example: the list is 1. Repairs & maintenance, 2. Bank fees. There's no R&M this year, and bank
   fees is $120, so bank fees shows $121.
5. **Fallback.** If no account on the list qualifies, the largest line in the section by absolute
   rounded amount absorbs it (ties go to the first in template order). The ReportDoc then carries a
   warning, so staff can extend the list.
6. **When totals conflict.** Rounding every total independently can leave totals that don't agree
   (income $100.40, expenses $50.60, profit $49.80: rounded 100 − 51 ≠ 50). Protected totals win, in
   this order: net profit, total assets, total equity (= net assets). A total that isn't protected
   (total income, total expenses, total liabilities, other subtotals) gives way. It shows whatever
   keeps the protected totals right, even if that's $1 off its own exact rounded figure. In the example,
   profit shows $50, and expenses show $50 via the absorbing line.
7. **Profit into equity.** The current-year profit shown in equity is the P&L's net profit exactly
   as shown.
8. **Drill-down.** Accounts behind a line always show their real balances. An absorbing line also shows
   a separate "rounding adjustment" entry, so the drill-down adds up to the line.
9. **Who sets the list.** The master user keeps a practice default list per entity type. Each new client
   gets a copy. Staff can reorder or edit a client's list. Changing the default doesn't alter existing
   clients unless someone runs the explicit "push default" command, which previews the change and only
   touches open years.

There's one implementation of this, in `acct-core`.

How it runs (`acct-core::reportdoc`), per column: lines start at their exact amount rounded;
then, statement by statement, links copy their already-settled target, groups (innermost first)
are brought to their exact total rounded, and totals are brought to theirs, protected ones first.
Once a protected row is settled, the lines under it are frozen, so nothing later can move it.
Protection is marked in the template (`protect: 1, 2, 3`), not hard-coded by name.

### Nil lines (provisional, to confirm)
A line that shows $0 in both columns is left out, along with any group whose lines are all left
out. Totals such as net profit always show. The accounts on a left-out line then don't appear in
drill-down, although they still count in the totals, so this needs the practice's confirmation.

### Statement amounts and columns (decided 2026-09-24)
- Negative amounts show in brackets: (1,234).
- A nil amount shows as a dash (–). A column with no amount at all, such as the prior column when
  there's no prior year, is blank.
- Amount columns are headed with the year the period ends and a dollar sign: "2026 $" and
  "2025 $". If both periods end in the same calendar year (a short or long year), the heading shows
  the full end date instead, such as "30 Sep 2026 $".
- When the prior year isn't finalised, a notice above the statements says so, and the prior column
  heading carries an asterisk with a footnote.
- **Part years (decided 2026-10-25).** A period that isn't exactly 12 months, such as a business's
  first year from its start date to 31 March, is headed "For the period ended …", not "For the year
  ended …". A full year keeps "year ended".
- **First years (confirmed 2026-10-25).** With no prior year there's no comparative column at all: it
  stays blank, and no synthetic prior year is invented.
- Drill-down shows each account's real balance in cents. The difference between the line's whole
  dollars and those balances shows as a separate rounding entry, so the drill-down adds up to the line.

### Depreciation (decided 2026-09-24)
Accounting depreciation only; tax depreciation is out of scope.

- **Methods.** `Dv`, `Sl` or `None`. A new asset class defaults to `Dv`.
- **SL basis.** SL runs on either a rate (basis points a year) or a useful life (whole months). The
  basis defaults to a rate. It follows the same cascade as the rate: practice class (master) → client
  override → the asset itself. With a life, a year's charge is (cost − residual) × 12 ÷ life months,
  prorated like a rate, so the asset writes off exactly at the end of its life.
- **Client overrides.** Staff, as well as the master user, may set a client's override of a practice
  asset class. Staff may also set custom settings on a single asset.
- **Years that aren't 12 months.** A full year's charge is scaled by the months in the client-year ÷ 12,
  so a 15-month year gets 15/12 of a year's charge.
- **Part-year conventions** (the year of acquisition):
  - `MonthsHeld { count_acquisition_month }`: whole calendar months from acquisition to year end ÷ 12.
    The acquisition month counts only if `count_acquisition_month` is set.
  - `Daily`: days held (acquisition day included) ÷ 365, or ÷ 366 if the client-year contains a
    29 February.
  - `FullYear`: a full year's charge (scaled for the year's length as above).
- **Disposal-year conventions:**
  - `None`: no depreciation in the year of disposal.
  - `ToDisposalDate`: depreciation up to the disposal date, prorated with the asset's part-year
    convention. Under `MonthsHeld` the disposal month counts exactly when the acquisition month does.
    Under `Daily` the disposal day counts. Under `FullYear` it's a full year's charge.
- **DV** charges rate × book value at the start of the year, or rate × cost in the year of acquisition,
  times the year's fraction. It never takes book value below zero.
- **SL** charges rate × (cost − residual) (or the life-based figure) times the year's fraction, and stops
  at the residual.
- **Rounding.** Each asset's charge for each year is computed exactly (i128) and rounded once to cents,
  half away from zero.
- **Additions** are not posted by the register. Purchases reach the ledger through TB import or journals.
  A reconciliation check compares the register with the linked ledger accounts and shows any difference.
- **Disposal.** Staff give the date, the proceeds and the account to debit with the proceeds, usually a
  suspense account where the client's books coded the sale receipt. The disposal journal, dated on the
  disposal date, debits that account with the proceeds and accumulated depreciation with the asset's
  total to the disposal date, credits cost, and puts the balance to the class's gain/loss account.
  Gain = proceeds − book value at the disposal date.
- **Asset schedule layout.** One block per class, then a total block, each with the current and prior
  columns like the statements:
  cost (opening, additions, disposals, closing), accumulated depreciation (opening, depreciation for
  the year, depreciation on disposals, closing), closing book value, and gain or loss on disposal.
  The wording is our own.
- **Schedule rounding** (revised 2026-09-24). Opening and closing book value and opening and closing
  accumulated depreciation each show their exact amount rounded. Cost is book value plus accumulated
  depreciation, so it can be $1 off its own exact amount rounded. Movements absorb any difference:
  additions for cost (disposals if there are no additions), and depreciation for the year for
  accumulated depreciation (depreciation on disposals if there's none). The total block is built the
  same way from exact totals. So its book value agrees with a balance sheet line that holds just the
  asset accounts, and its depreciation for the year with a P&L line that holds just the depreciation
  accounts, unless those lines absorbed statement rounding. (Two earlier versions each broke one of
  these; the fixture's FY2026 showed it. Exact: cost 41,956.28, additions 23,456.78, depreciation
  7,284.61, accumulated 10,059.54, book value 31,896.74. Shown: 41,957, 23,457, 7,285, 10,060 and
  31,897.)

### Account codes (decided 2026-09-23)
- A code is one or more segments separated by `.`, such as `200`, `200.01`, `1100` or `A100`. Segments
  are ASCII letters and digits, and codes are at most 20 characters. Letters are stored uppercase, so
  `a100` and `A100` are the same code.
- Codes sort segment by segment. An all-digit segment compares as a number, so `200` < `200.01` < `1100`,
  and a digit segment sorts before a segment containing letters. A code that is a prefix of another sorts
  first. `0200` and `200` are different codes; the shorter one sorts first.
- A mapping range `from`–`to` includes both ends, and `to` also covers its own sub-accounts: `200`–`299`
  includes `299.05`. Ranges may not overlap, and touching ends such as `100`–`199` and `199`–`250`
  count as overlapping.
- The master user builds master charts and templates. The practice ships premade charts in the familiar
  NZ style (similar code ranges and groupings to common small-business charts), written in our own
  words and not copied from any vendor (hard rule 3). Staff can reorganise a client's own chart.
- **Starter sets** (added 2026-10-26): `install_starter_set` puts the shipped company or sole-trader
  defaults (master chart, statement template, mapping and asset classes, from `fixtures/practice/`) onto
  an entity type that has none of them yet. It's refused if any are there, since defaults never reach
  back. A practice admin can also make a master chart for another entity type, empty or as a copy.
- **Editing a master chart**: a practice admin adds accounts, renames them, sets their default GST code
  and makes them active or inactive, as staff do for a client's chart. Edits reach clients set up
  afterwards, never ones already copied. A master account stays active while a practice asset class or
  merchant entry uses it, or while setting up a business needs it.
- **Editing templates and mappings** (added 2026-10-26): a practice admin edits a template's
  statements, rows (groups, lines, totals, links), signs, rounding protection and checks, and a
  mapping's code ranges. Each save is a new version. The latest version of every mapping always fits
  the latest version of its template: a template revision that would leave a mapping pointing at a
  removed line is refused unless it revises that mapping in the same command. A new entity type sets
  up its first template and mapping as copies of another type's, or blank.
- **Moving a year onto a newer layout**: years keep the template and mapping versions they were set up
  with. `move_year_layout` moves one open year onto the latest versions of its mapping (or another
  mapping for its entity type) and that mapping's template, after a preview of every statement row it
  changes. It's refused if the statements wouldn't build that way. Its comparatives follow, since
  they're always presented through the year's own layout. Finalised years never move.

### The conservative figure and Save tax (decided 2026-10-19)
- **The rule.** "Tax to pay if submitted" is never lower than what IRD would ask for on the statements
  uploaded. Every unknown is assumed to cost the most until the owner answers it in Save tax.
- **Starting assumptions.** Save tax removes each one:

  | Until the owner says otherwise | Effect on the figure |
  |---|---|
  | Every deposit is business income, GST-inclusive at the standard rate if the owner is GST registered | Output GST and income tax on all of it |
  | Every withdrawal is personal | Nothing claimed |
  | The owner has other income, so the business income is taxed at the top rate | 39% on all of it instead of the bands |
  | The owner has a student loan, and other income already over its threshold | 12% of all of it |
  | Nothing is already paid or credited (PAYE, RWT, provisional tax, IETC) | Nothing deducted |
  | Where the balance rose across a gap between statements, the rise was income | See "Gaps" below |

- **What the owner sees first.** One figure, with one line under it: "Counts every deposit as income and
  claims nothing. Save tax to bring it down." Opening the figure breaks it down by return (each GST period,
  each income year), each marked "can file now" or "can file after" a date.
- **What it covers.** Every GST period and income year with uploaded lines that hasn't been filed,
  including the open ones, which are marked "so far". It never leaves out tax owed just because the return
  can't be filed yet.
- **Save tax order.** One queue of cards, sorted by the saving each would make now, largest first, and
  re-sorted after every answer, because the tax bands make savings depend on order. A card that would
  save nothing is hidden. Question cards and M11's categorising share the queue. The questions:
  - "Is this business your only income?" Yes: the bands apply from zero. No: the owner enters the other
    income and the tax deducted from it, or M14 prefills both from IRD.
  - "Do you have a student loan?"
  - Likely transfers between the owner's own accounts, which are paired on import and applied only once
    confirmed.
  - Payments to IRD found in the statements: which were income tax for this year (a credit), and which
    were GST.
  - Possible duplicates, and missing statements (gaps) with what each gap is costing.
  - The independent earner tax credit, offered only when income is between $24,000 and $70,000.

### Bank statements to the ledger (decided 2026-10-19)
- **Every imported line posts straight away**, one journal per line (kind `bank_line`), dated with the
  bank's posting date. Recoding in M11 reverses and reposts that one journal. M15's GL listing hides
  matched reversal pairs, which is enough.
- **A deposit** debits the bank account and credits 140 *Money in, not yet sorted*. If the owner is GST
  registered on that date, it's split: GST is amount × 1500 ÷ 11500, rounded half away from zero to cents,
  credited to 810 *GST*, and the rest goes to 140.
- **A withdrawal** debits 965 *Money out, not yet sorted*, an equity account, so nothing is claimed. It
  isn't split for GST.
- **Holding accounts.** 140 and 965 are ordinary accounts in the sole-trader chart, recorded on the
  business as its "not yet sorted" accounts. Owners never see their names, only "not yet sorted".
  Accountants see them as their own lines in the TB and statements, and M15's finalise pre-check stops on
  a balance in either.
- **Start date.** The business start date. For a business that started before its earliest uploaded
  income year, it's that year's first day.
- **Opening balance**, posted once per bank account on its first upload, dated the start date. It
  credits 950 *Owner's capital* if the business started that year, or 970 *Owner's equity* if it started
  earlier.
  - If the first statement begins on the start date, the opening balance is the balance before its first
    line. That's the statement's opening balance; or the first running balance less the first line; or,
    for OFX, the closing balance less the lines.
  - Otherwise the owner is asked once for the balance on the start date, and the days up to the first
    statement are a gap.
- **Gaps.** A gap is a run of days between the start date and the latest statement that no statement
  covers.
  - If the balance at both ends is known, a *gap journal* posts the difference on the gap's last day: a
    rise is treated like a deposit (140, plus GST), a fall like a withdrawal (965).
  - A gap journal is a system journal. It is reversed or regenerated when a statement covering the gap
    arrives.
  - If either end's balance is unknown, the gap is flagged with no amount, and the figure carries a notice
    that it's incomplete.
- **Transfers between the owner's own accounts** aren't netted automatically. The importer pairs likely
  ones: the same amount with opposite signs, in two of the business's accounts, posted at most 3 days
  apart.
  - Until the owner confirms a pair, its two lines stay as income and drawings.
  - Confirming reposts both lines as one transfer between the bank accounts.
- **Other withdrawals that aren't spending**, such as transfers out, loan repayments and payments to IRD,
  stay in 965 until sorted.
- **Which client-year a line belongs to:** the one containing its date. A sole trader's client-years run
  from 1 April to 31 March, and the first starts on the business start date.
  - Import creates any missing year between the business start and the line's date.
  - Lines dated before the business start date aren't imported; the owner is shown them, with the reason.
  - A line dated after today is refused.
- **Books source.** Every sole trader is bank-coded. A business moving from Xero part-way through a year is
  M16's question. It needs an ADR, because a cut-over TB plus bank lines bends the books-source rule.

### GST (decided 2026-10-19)
- **Rate.** The rate is versioned data in core, keyed by date, not a practice setting: 1500 bp (15%) for
  dates from 1 October 2010. A date with no rate gets no GST figure, and the screen says why.
- **Registration.** Setup asks whether the owner is registered and from which date. There's no GST before
  that date, and the first period runs from that date to the end of the period it falls in.
  - An unregistered owner sees no GST. If their deposits in any 12 months exceed $60,000, a notice says
    they may have to register (**check**: the threshold came from non-IRD sources).
  - Deregistering is backlog.
- **Basis.** M10 supports the payments basis only, which IRD allows up to $2 million of sales in 12
  months.
  - Setup turns down the invoice and hybrid bases with a plain reason: bank statements don't show unpaid
    invoices, so the figure couldn't stay conservative.
  - Invoice basis waits for sales invoicing (backlog).
- **Filing frequency and periods.** M10 supports a 31 March balance date only:
  - monthly
  - two-monthly, ending in odd months (January, March, May, July, September, November), which IRD gives for
    a 31 March balance date; even months are allowed if that's what IRD has on record
  - six-monthly, ending 30 September and 31 March. This is allowed under $500,000 of GST-exclusive sales in
    12 months, with a notice if deposits exceed that.

  Source: IRD, [Which GST accounting basis and filing frequency should I
  use](https://www.ird.govt.nz/gst/registering-for-gst/which-gst-accounting-basis-and-filing-frequency-should-i-use)
  (updated 12 Aug 2026).
- **Due dates.** M10 doesn't show them. M14 takes them from IRD (`RetrieveFilingObligation`), so we never
  encode weekends and holidays.
  - For reference: the 28th of the next month; 7 May for a period ending 31 March; 15 January for one
    ending 30 November ([IRD](https://www.ird.govt.nz/gst/filing-and-paying-gst-and-refunds)).
- **Which period a line falls in:** its posting date, the date that drives the running balance.
- **Output tax.** Every deposit from the registration date counts as GST-inclusive at the standard rate.
  In M11 the owner can mark deposits as not income, exempt (interest, for example) or zero-rated.
- **The return**, computed on period totals as the GST return form lays out:

  | Box | What it holds | M10 value |
  |---|---|---|
  | 5 | Sales and income in the period, GST-inclusive, zero-rated included | Total deposits |
  | 6 | Zero-rated supplies included in box 5 | 0 |
  | 7 | Box 5 − box 6 | |
  | 8 | Box 7 × 1500 ÷ 11500 (that is, 3/23) | |
  | 9 | Adjustments | 0 |
  | 10 | GST collected: box 8 + box 9 | |
  | 11 | Purchases and expenses with a standard GST code, GST-inclusive | 0 |
  | 12 | Box 11 × 3/23 | 0 |
  | 13 | Credit adjustments | 0 |
  | 14 | GST credit: box 12 + box 13 | 0 |
  | 15 | Box 10 − box 14: to pay if positive, a refund if negative | The M10 GST figure |

  - Boxes 8 and 12 round half away from zero to cents. IRD doesn't say how to round them, so that's our
    choice (**check**).
  - The box layout comes from a secondary summary of the GST101A (**check** against M14's build pack).
  - The 3/23 calculation is IRD's
    ([IRD GST workshop](https://www.ird.govt.nz/about-us/videos/webinars/gst-workshop/registering)).
- **Ledger against return.**
  - The ledger splits GST per line, so its GST balance can differ by a few cents from box 10 − box 14. The
    worked example below is 1 cent out.
  - When a return is filed (M14), a system journal clears the difference to 295 *GST rounding*.
  - Until then, the figure uses the return's number.
- **Input tax.** None in M10. In M11, a withdrawal categorised with a standard GST code claims its GST,
  split per line. The records rules, from IRD's
  [taxable supply information](https://www.ird.govt.nz/gst/tax-invoices-for-gst/how-taxable-supply-information-for-gst-works)
  page:
  - Up to $200, the bank line is enough.
  - Over $200, the owner confirms they hold the supplier's taxable supply information before the GST is
    claimed. It's one tick per line, with an option to confirm all at once.
  - Over $1,000, that information must also name the owner.

  The tiers come from secondary summaries (**check**).
- **Over $200, not yet confirmed (decided 2026-10-23).** The expense is still GST-exclusive. The GST
  waits in 615 *GST to claim, records not yet held*, an asset, and isn't in box 11 until confirmed.
  Confirming moves it from 615 to 810. The GST figure then falls, and income tax doesn't change.
  - Why not put the whole amount in the expense: GST the owner is entitled to claim is still input tax,
    and section DB 2 of the Income Tax Act 2007 denies an income tax deduction for input tax. Lacking
    the paperwork delays the claim; it doesn't turn the GST into a cost. Deducting it would put the
    income tax figure below what IRD would ask for, which breaks the conservative rule. It would also
    give the owner both benefits once they confirm (a deduction in one year, input tax in a later
    period), unless the expense was changed back, possibly in a filed or finalised year.
  - When the owner confirms after the line's GST return has been filed, the claim goes in the return
    being prepared when they confirm, not the filed one (**check**: how late a claim can be made, and
    in which box; M14).
  - If the owner says they'll never get the information, the GST in 615 becomes part of the expense.
    That's a real cost, so it's deductible (**check**).
  - The accountant's finalise pre-check (M15) lists any balance left in 615.
- **Income tax treatment.** For a registered owner, income and expenses are GST-exclusive. GST collected,
  GST paid to IRD and GST refunds are never income or expenses. A GST refund deposit counts as income in
  M10 only because nothing is sorted yet; M11 marks it.

### Income tax for sole traders (decided 2026-10-19)
- **Years.** M10 supports the 2026 income year (1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026) and the 2027 income year
  (1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027), with a 31 March balance date only.
  - A year with no rate table shows no income tax figure, and the screen says so. We never guess a rate.
  - Older years import into the ledger as normal.
  - Backlog: 2025's composite table, for late filers, and non-standard balance dates.
- **Rate table.** Bands apply to taxable income in cents. Each rate applies to income above From, up to
  and including To:

  | Income years | From (cents) | To (cents) | Rate (bp) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 2026, 2027 | 0 | 1,560,000 | 1050 |
  | | 1,560,000 | 5,350,000 | 1750 |
  | | 5,350,000 | 7,810,000 | 3000 |
  | | 7,810,000 | 18,000,000 | 3300 |
  | | 18,000,000 | (none) | 3900 |

  - Source: IRD, [Tax rates for
    individuals](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-individuals/tax-codes-and-tax-rates-for-individuals/tax-rates-for-individuals),
    the "From 1 April 2025" table (page updated 3 Jun 2025, read 2026-10-19).
  - IRD writes the bands in whole dollars ("0 – $15,600", "$15,601 – $53,500"). We read them as "up to
    $15,600.00, then above it".
  - 2027 uses the same table. IRD's page gives it with no end date, and Budget 2026 made no change
    ([RSM](https://www.rsm.global/newzealand/news/budget-2026-closer-look-tax-measures); **check** when the
    2027 IR3 build pack arrives).
- **Computing it.** Each band's tax is computed exactly in `i128`. The bands are summed, then rounded once
  to cents, half away from zero (**check** against IRD's test cases in M14).
- **Taxable income** is the year's income accounts less its expense accounts, from the ledger, and
  GST-exclusive for a registered owner. In the conservative position, that's every deposit after GST plus
  any rising gap, less nothing.
- **The conservative rate.** 39% flat, until the owner says the business is their only income.
  - After that, the bands apply.
  - If they have other income, they enter it and the tax deducted from it (M11), or M14 prefills both from
    IRD. The bands then run on total income, and the tax deducted is a credit.
- **What "Tax to pay if submitted" includes:**

  | Item | In the figure? | Rule and source |
  |---|---|---|
  | GST for each unfiled period | In | Above |
  | Income tax | In | Above |
  | Student loan | In, until the owner says they have none | 12% of all taxable business income until "only income" is answered. Then 12% of income over the threshold: $24,128 for 2026 ([IRD](https://www.ird.govt.nz/se-repay-student-loan)). The 2027 threshold is said to be unchanged by non-IRD sources (**check**). |
  | PAYE and RWT already deducted | Out, until entered (M11) or prefilled (M14) | Leaving them out only overstates the figure. Interest arrives net of RWT, and taxing the net amount with no credit is still at least the true tax. |
  | Income tax already paid (provisional or terminal) | Out, until confirmed | M11 card from IRD payments found in the statements; M14 from IRD |
  | Independent earner tax credit | Out, until the owner confirms eligibility | $520 a year, reduced by 13c per dollar over $66,000, gone at $70,000, worked out on whole months. Not available with Working for Families, an income-tested benefit or NZ Super ([IRD](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-individuals/individual-tax-credits/independent-earner-tax-credit-ietc)). |
  | ACC levies | Out | ACC invoices them, not IRD. Levies paid are an expense category in M11. An estimate is backlog. |
  | Next year's provisional tax | Out | After filing, a notice when residual income tax is over $5,000 (**check**; backlog: provisional tax) |
  | Late filing penalties and interest | Out | Later, with M14's due dates |
  | Donations, Working for Families, KiwiSaver, child support | Out | Not part of the IR3 figure |

- **Answers and credits (M11, decided 2026-10-23).** The owner's answers are kept per income year
  (`YearAnswers` in `acct_core::tax::income_tax`); a year with nothing answered costs the most.
  - *Other income.* Once the owner says the business isn't their only income and enters the other income
    and the PAYE/RWT deducted from it, the bands run on business plus other income, and the deducted tax
    is a credit. Until both are entered, the business income stays at 39% flat.
  - *When the figure rises.* If less tax was deducted from the other income than that income's own tax
    (untaxed rental income, say), the figure goes above the conservative one. That's right, because IRD
    would ask for it, so the figure isn't capped (owner's call, 2026-10-23). This is the one answer that
    can raise the figure; its Save tax card shows a negative saving.
  - *Student loan with other income.* 12% of the business income's share of total income over the
    threshold: (total − threshold, if positive) − (other income − threshold, if positive). The other
    income's share is taken to have had repayments deducted at source (**check**).
  - *Income tax already paid* (provisional or terminal, confirmed by the owner) is a credit.
  - *Credits come off income tax only*, and a year's income tax never goes below nil: **the figure never
    shows a refund** (owner's call, 2026-10-23). The student loan is added after.
- **Independent earner tax credit (IETC).** From 31 July 2024, so for every year we hold rates for:

  | | Amount |
  |---|---|
  | Income from | $24,000.00 (inclusive) |
  | Full credit | $520 a year ($10 a week), up to $66,000 |
  | Abatement | 13c for each complete dollar of income over $66,000 |
  | Income to | $70,000 (fully abated there) |
  | Part years | Whole months only: a month with any Working for Families, income-tested benefit, NZ Super, Veteran's Pension or an overseas equivalent doesn't count |

  - Income means total income before tax (business plus other), with no losses brought forward.
  - We compute ($520 − abatement) × eligible months ÷ 12, rounded once half away from zero to cents.
    That order follows the Taxation (Budget Measures) Act 2024's transitional formula for 2024–25
    ("(credit − full year abatement) × days ÷ 365", s LC 14); the "complete dollar" wording is from the same
    Act (**check** both against the current s LC 13 and IRD's test cases).
  - It's given only once total income is known (the "only income" question answered and any other
    income entered) and the owner has said how many months they're eligible.
  - The $24,000 start is a cliff: $23,999.99 gets nothing and $24,000.00 gets $520, so a cent more
    income can mean less tax. That's the law, and the only place the figure isn't monotonic in income.
  - Sources: IRD, [Independent earner tax credit
    (IETC)](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-individuals/individual-tax-credits/independent-earner-tax-credit-ietc)
    (updated 31 Mar 2025, read 2026-10-23); the Taxation (Budget Measures) Act 2024, inserting ss LC 14
    and LC 15 into the Income Tax Act 2007, as quoted in search results (legislation.govt.nz refused our
    fetch, so **check** the text).
- **Where it's computed.** `acct_core::tax::position` takes in-memory inputs (each GST period's totals,
  each income year's taxable income, the answers) and returns every return and the total.
  `position::saving` prices one change (an answer, a period's totals, a year's income, or a recoding's
  moves) as the fall in the total, which is how Save tax sorts its cards. The server only loads the
  inputs.

- **Part years.** Bands are annual and never prorated for a sole trader: a business that starts in August
  still files the whole income year. The figure covers the lines uploaded so far and doesn't project to
  the year end. An open year is labelled "so far".
- **Depreciation.** M10 claims nothing, so tax depreciation doesn't come up.
  - M11 puts asset purchases into the M4 register at accounting rates no higher than IRD's rate for that
    kind of asset, with each rate's source recorded. Depreciation in the ledger is then never more than tax
    allows.
- **IR10.** IRD lists the IR10 among the forms needed with an IR3 "for untaxed income from your business"
  ([IRD](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-individuals/what-happens-at-the-end-of-the-tax-year/individual-income-tax-return---ir3/complete-my-individual-income-tax-return---ir3)).
  IRD's [tax technical article](https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/general-articles/revised-ir10-financial-statements-summary)
  says it isn't mandatory, though preferred. CLAUDE.md keeps it out of scope.
  - M14 checks the build pack. If the IR3 needs it, an ADR brings it in. Until then there's no IR10
    mapping.

### Accounting policies and notes (decided 2026-10-25)
The practice keeps a paragraph library per entity type, in our own words (hard rule 3). Each
client-year picks paragraphs and keeps its own copies, which staff can edit; the notes the statements
show are those copies with the client's fields filled in. Asked and answered on 2026-10-25.

- **Layout.** The notes follow the statements under the heading "Notes to the financial statements".
  - Note 1 is "Statement of accounting policies". Every paragraph placed as a *policy* goes inside it,
    under its own title as a sub-heading, in the order the year picked them.
  - Every paragraph placed as a *note* is a numbered note of its own (2, 3, …), in picked order. With no
    policies picked, the first note is 1.
  - The text is plain: no emoji or formatting. A blank line in a paragraph starts a new paragraph of
    text; a single line break stays a line break, which is how lists such as the depreciation rates
    show.
- **Fields.** `{business_name}` (the client's name), `{period_start}` and `{balance_date}` (the year's
  first and last days, written "1 April 2025" and "31 March 2026"), and `{depreciation_methods}` (one
  line per asset class held in the year, in the register's order, each listing its distinct methods:
  "Tools and equipment: 25% diminishing value", "Vehicles: 10.75% straight line; 30% diminishing
  value", "Fitout: straight line over 5 years", "Art: not depreciated"). `{{` and `}}` write a literal
  brace.
  - A field the notes don't know, or a stray brace, is refused when a paragraph or copy is saved.
  - A field with nothing to fill it, such as the depreciation policy in a year with no assets, fails
    the year's report with `notes_failed`, naming the paragraph and field. A note never goes out with a
    gap. The fix is to unpick that paragraph or edit the year's copy.
- **Starter set for sole traders** (`fixtures/practice/paragraphs.json`; formal wording chosen over a
  plainer alternative):
  - Policies: basis of preparation (special purpose statements for the owner and the owner's income
    tax return, at historical cost); GST, in two versions, registered and not registered, of which a
    year picks one; depreciation, with the rates; going concern; changes in accounting policies.
  - Notes: income tax (the tax is the owner's, so there's no income tax expense, and tax paid from the
    business shows in owner's equity); owner's equity (capital introduced, drawings, and money out not
    yet sorted).
  - The two extras, changes in accounting policies and owner's equity, were drafted after the others
    and still need the practice to read them (`NON-CODE-TODOS.md`).
- **Defaults never reach back.**
  - Revising a library paragraph writes a new version; no year's copy changes.
  - `push_paragraph` brings the latest version onto open years, after its preview
    (`GET /api/paragraphs/{id}/push-preview`). It never touches a finalised year.
  - A copy staff have edited is kept as it is: the preview lists it as "kept: edited for this client",
    so staff can update it by hand. (Chosen over overwriting edited copies.)
  - A new year starts with copies of its neighbouring year's notes, edits included.
  - `remove_paragraph` takes a paragraph out of the library. Years that picked it keep their copies
    (and their reports keep the note); a year may keep or reorder such a copy, but once dropped it
    can't be picked again. A removed paragraph can't be revised or pushed. Its key is free to add
    again as a new paragraph, but a year can't hold the old copy and the new paragraph together.
- **Finalised years.** Picking or editing notes in a finalised year is refused. The notes are part of
  the ReportDoc, so the snapshot holds exactly the wording that was signed.

### Bank statement formats (decided 2026-10-19)
- **Order.**
  1. OFX first. It gives every line an id and gives a closing balance, and most NZ banks offer it
     (**check** per bank).
  2. A CSV parser per bank, the largest banks first: ANZ, ASB, BNZ, Westpac, Kiwibank.
  3. QIF.

  Revisit the order once we know the first customers' banks.
- **Real layouts.** A bank's CSV layout can't be written from memory.
  - For each bank, someone with an account there exports a statement and copies only the layout into a
    synthetic sample with invented values (hard rule 1). The layout is the preamble, headers, date and
    amount formats, and encoding.
  - The real export never enters the repo. A local-only check in `tools/` runs the parser over it on that
    person's own machine.
  - Until then, the fixtures' two invented layouts stand in.
- **Each CSV parser records:**
  - the preamble and header rows
  - one signed amount column, or separate debit and credit columns
  - whether there's a running balance
  - the text columns, and the order they're joined in
  - the date format and encoding
  - where the account number is
- **OFX** 1.x in SGML (unclosed tags) and 2.x in XML: a bank statement (`BANKMSGSRSV1`) or a credit card
  statement (`CREDITCARDMSGSRSV1`), one per file.
- **QIF.** One `!Type:Bank` or `!Type:CCard` section, with day-first dates (dd/mm/yyyy, d/m/yy). A file
  whose dates only make sense month first is refused. A card file is read as `qif-ccard-1`.
- **Statement kind.** Every statement is `bank` or `credit_card`, decided by the file (OFX message set,
  QIF section, or the CSV layout). A file holding both is refused.
- **Credit cards (added 2026-10-23, M11).** NZD cards only.
  - **Signs, from the card's point of view,** the same as a bank account: positive is money in. A
    purchase, fee or interest charge is negative (money out); a payment to the card or a refund is
    positive (money in); a balance owed is negative, because the card is a liability. OFX and QIF
    already sign cards this way and are read as is. A CSV that writes amounts the way the bill does
    (charges unsigned, credits marked `CR`) is flipped by its parser, so every statement reaches the
    server signed the same way.
  - **Card numbers are never kept whole.** The card's identity is its last four digits, as
    `XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-1234`, whatever mask the file used; a full card number in a file is masked on
    reading. Two cards with the same last four digits would look the same, so the owner says which
    card a file is for when that happens. Descriptions are kept as written (card fragments in them
    tell transactions apart).
  - Each card gets its own liability account (PLAN.md M11); that's the server's job, not the parser's.
- **Foreign currency.** Foreign-currency accounts are out (multi-currency is out of scope), and refused
  with a message. A foreign purchase on an NZD card is fine: the line is in NZD.
- **Versions.** Each parser has an id and a version, such as `kowhai-csv-1`, and the right one is found by
  inspecting the file.
  - When a bank changes its layout, we add a version and keep the old one, so old files still read.
  - When no parser matches, the owner sees "We can't read this file", the formats we read, and how to
    export OFX. Nothing is stored.
- **Duplicates.** The key is account, posting date, amount, normalised description, and an occurrence
  count.
  - The account is the bank account number, or for a card the masked card number.
  - The normalised description is the text fields joined in the parser's order with single spaces: OFX
    `NAME` then `MEMO`; QIF `P` then `M`; CSV per parser. It's uppercased, with runs of spaces collapsed
    and the ends trimmed.
  - Nothing else is stripped. Reference numbers and card fragments tell real transactions apart.
  - When unsure, keep the line and offer it as a possible duplicate in Save tax. A duplicate deposit only
    overstates the figure; a dropped one would understate it.

### Importing from Xero and MYOB (M16, parsers written 2026-10-26; a person still has to check them against real exports)
Every history importer reads one export file and returns the same source-neutral shape,
`acct_import::history`:
- the chart (code, name, type, default tax code);
- a TB at one date;
- GL detail for a period (each account's opening balance, then its postings);
- or contacts.

Rules for all sources:
- Codes are kept exactly as the source writes them. Mapping them onto the client's chart comes later,
  with a preview.
- Amounts are integer cents, debit positive. Brackets and a leading `-` both mean negative, and
  thousands separators must be in groups of three (`1,23.00` is rejected, never guessed).
- Each source type collapses onto asset, liability, equity, income, cost of sales, expense, other
  income or other expense. The source's own type is kept beside it.
- Problems are reported per row, with the column, the value and the reason, all at once. A TB whose
  rows don't sum to zero is rejected.
- **GL against TB.** For each account, the TB balance at the end of a period must equal the GL's
  opening balance plus the sum of its postings (`history::gl_differences`). Codes are compared
  verbatim.

#### Xero
`acct_import::xero::parse` works out which export a file is by inspecting it, in this order: chart,
contacts, GL, TB.
- **Headers.** Columns are found by name, never by position. Matching ignores case, spaces, `-`, `_`,
  and the `*` Xero puts on required columns in its import templates.
- **Codes.** A Xero account may have no code; bank accounts often don't. We reject such an account
  with "give it one in Xero and export again", because without a code nothing can be matched across
  files.

The four exports:
- **Chart of accounts** (Accounting › Chart of accounts › Export). Its columns match Xero's import
  template: `*Code, *Name, *Type, *Tax Code, Description, Dashboard, Expense Claims, Enable Payments,
  Balance`.
  - We read the code, name, type and tax code, and a `Status` column if there is one (`Archived`
    makes the account inactive).
  - Types are read by display name or API code: Bank, Current Asset, Fixed Asset, Inventory,
    Non-current Asset and Prepayment are assets. Current Liability, Liability, Non-current Liability
    and PAYG Liability are liabilities. Equity is equity. Revenue and Sales are income. Other Income
    is other income. Direct Costs is cost of sales. Expense, Overhead and Depreciation are expenses.
- **Trial Balance** (Reports › Trial Balance › Export).
  - It starts with a preamble: the title, the organisation, and `As at <date>`, which gives the
    balance date. Then comes the header row.
  - The header is either `Account Code, Account, Account Type, Debit, Credit, YTD Debit, YTD Credit`,
    or `Account, Debit, Credit, YTD Debit, YTD Credit` with the code in the account cell (`Sales (200)`
    or `200 - Sales`).
  - The YTD columns hold the balance at the date, so we use them whenever they're there. Debit and
    Credit alone hold the month's movement.
  - Section headings and `Total` rows are skipped.
- **Account Transactions** (Reports › Account Transactions › Export): the GL detail.
  - It starts with a preamble whose `For the period <from> to <to>` line gives the period.
  - Then a header row: `Date, Source, Description, Reference, Debit, Credit, Running Balance, Gross,
    GST`.
  - By default the rows are grouped by account: a heading row (`Sales (200)`), `Opening Balance`, the
    postings, `Total …` and `Closing Balance`. Rows may instead be ungrouped, with `Account Code` and
    `Account` columns on every row.
  - An opening balance comes from Debit and Credit, or from the running balance (debit positive) when
    those are blank.
  - Totals and closing balances are skipped, since the TB check covers them.
  - **Retained earnings.** Xero closes the year virtually. The next year's TB shows retained earnings
    with no postings behind it, so the GL's opening balance for that account has to carry it.
- **Contacts** (Contacts › All contacts › Export).
  - The columns follow Xero's contact import template (`ContactName`, `EmailAddress`, `PhoneNumber`,
    `TaxNumber`, address columns, default accounts…). We read the name, email, phone (`PhoneNumber`,
    then mobile, then DDI) and GST number (`TaxNumber`).
  - The export has no customer or supplier flag. A sales default (account or tax rate) makes a
    contact a customer, a purchases default makes it a supplier, both make it both, and neither
    leaves it "other".
- **Dates** are read as `1 Apr 2025`, `01 April 2025`, `01/04/2025` or `2025-04-01`.

Sources (the Xero Central links couldn't be fetched without a browser on 2026-10-26, so a person should open them):

#### MYOB
This covers MYOB AccountRight (desktop) and MYOB Business (browser), not MYOB AO Classic (see M16).
`acct_import::myob::detect` and `parse` find the header row by name, up to 20 rows below a report's
title rows. They check for cards, contacts, GL, TB, then accounts.
- **Files.** Exports are tab or comma separated text; tab wins if any of the first rows has one. Blank
  rows, a BOM and the `{}` first row of MYOB's import templates are skipped. Header names ignore case
  and spacing, MYOB's `*` (required) and `^` (matching) markers, and a trailing `:`.
- **Account numbers** are kept verbatim (`1-1110`), up to 10 characters of letters, digits, `-`, `.`
  and `/`. AccountRight's are five digits with an optional separator; MYOB Business allows ten.
  Their first digit is MYOB's classification: 1 asset, 2 liability, 3 equity, 4 income, 5 cost of
  sales, 6 expense, 8 other income, 9 other expense. We fall back on it when a type is blank, and
  use it to tell which side a report's balance is on.
- **Amounts** may have `$` (`-$12.00` and `$-12.00` are both negative). Zeros beyond two decimals are
  dropped; any other third decimal is rejected, not rounded.
- **Dates** are day first, as MYOB writes them in New Zealand: `1/04/2024` and `01/04/24`, plus Excel's
  `1-Apr-2024` and `1 Apr 2024`, and `2024-04-01`. US-style `04/31/2024` is rejected.
- **Source.** An accounts list with `Parent Account Number`, or a contacts report, is MYOB Business;
  everything else is reported as AccountRight. The two products' TB and GL reports look the same.

The exports:
- **Accounts list.** In AccountRight it's File › Export Data › Accounts. The user picks the fields,
  and Match All takes them all, starting `Account Number, Account Name, Header, Balance, Account Type,
  …, GST Code (NZ), …, Inactive Account`. In MYOB Business it's Settings › Import and export data ›
  Export › Chart of accounts, a tab-separated `.txt` with `Account Number, Account Name, Account Type,
  Header, Parent Account Number, Tax Code/GST Code, Inactive Account`.
  - Only the number is required. Types are read by name in either product, ignoring case:
    - Assets: Asset, Bank, Accounts Receivable, Other Current Asset, Fixed Asset, Other Asset.
    - Liabilities: Liability, Credit Card, Accounts Payable, Other Current Liability, Long Term
      Liability, Other Liability.
    - The rest: Equity, Income, Cost of Sales, Expense, Other Income, Other Expense.
  - `Header` and `Inactive Account` are one-character flags: blank or `N` is off, anything else
    (`H`, `Y`) is on. Header accounts are kept and marked. AccountRight's help says any non-blank
    value marks a header, and MYOB Business's says `H`. MYOB Business's help is unclear about a blank
    `Inactive Account`; we follow AccountRight's (blank is active).
  - The tax code comes from the column named `GST Code` or `Tax Code` (not the freight one).
  - `Balance` is ignored: it's the balance on the day of the export. History comes from the TB.
- **Trial Balance** report, exported as CSV or TSV from AccountRight. MYOB Business exports reports
  only as Excel or PDF, so the user saves the Excel file as CSV.
  - Title rows come first. Their last date is the balance date: `As of 31/03/2025`, or a month on
    its own (`March 2025`) meaning its last day.
  - Columns are `Debit, Credit` (the month) and `YTD Debit, YTD Credit` (the year to date). The YTD
    columns win when present.
  - MYOB leaves the `Account No.` column off this report by default. Without it, we take the number
    from an account cell that starts with one (`1-1110 Business Cheque Account`). If neither has a
    number, the row is rejected with "add the Account No. column to the report before exporting".
  - `Total` rows and headings without amounts are skipped. A repeated account is rejected.
- **General Ledger [Detail]** report, exported the same way. The title rows' first and last dates are
  the period. The columns are `ID No., Src, Date, Memo, Debit, Credit, Name`, plus the account and,
  if shown, `Ending Balance`.
  - The report is grouped by account. The heading row names the account (`Account No.` and
    `Account Name`, or a combined account cell) and carries its beginning balance; a separate
    `Beginning Balance` row under it also works. Then come the postings (any row with a date) and a
    `Total` row, which is skipped.
  - A file that repeats the account on every posting row, as people do to flatten the Excel export,
    reads the same.
  - A posting is `Debit − Credit`. Its reference is `ID No.`, its source is `Src` (`CR`, `CD`, `GJ`,
    `SJ`, `PJ`), and its description is `Memo`, or the card's `Name` when the memo is blank.
  - The beginning balance comes from `Debit`/`Credit` when either is filled. Otherwise it comes from
    the balance column, read as positive on the account's normal side: a credit for 2, 3, 4 and 8
    accounts. **Assumed, not documented**: MYOB's help doesn't say how this report signs balances.
    When `Ending Balance` is there, every posting row is checked against the running balance, so a
    file that signs balances another way fails loudly, row by row, instead of importing wrong
    openings.
  - An account that appears twice, or a posting before any account heading, is rejected.
- **Cards** (AccountRight, File › Export Data › Cards). Customer, supplier and personal cards come as
  separate files, and only the fields tell them apart. Customer-only fields (`% Monthly Charge`,
  `Receipt Memo`, `Salesperson`, `Price Level`, `Use Customer's GST Code`) make a customer file, and
  supplier-only fields (`Cost Per Hour`, `Payment Memo`, `Use Supplier's GST Code`,
  `Particulars (NZ only)`) make a supplier file. Neither makes them other contacts.
  - The name is `Co./Last Name`, with `First Name` in front for an individual.
  - We also read `GST ID No.`, the email (`Email` or `Addr 1 - Email`) and the phone (`Phone No. 1`
    or `Addr 1 - Phone No. 1`).
- **Contacts** (MYOB Business). It has no contacts export, so its Contacts report goes to Excel and
  is saved as CSV. We read a name, `Contact type` (Customer, Supplier, both, or Personal/Other), email,
  phone and GST number. **Assumed, not documented**: the column names.

## Worked example: the sole trader's statements (worked 2026-10-25; a person still has to check it)
This is M15's check that the P&L and balance sheet are right for a sole trader. It starts from the
ledger the Save tax example above ends with (`expected.tb` in `fixtures/clients/marsh-hollow-coding.json`)
and lays it out with the sole-trader template and mapping (`fixtures/practice/sole-trader-*`). Claude worked
it by hand and cross-checked it with a separate script that doesn't use `acct-core`. The golden test is
`crates/core/tests/sole_trader_statements.rs`, which snapshots the ReportDoc JSON and asserts the figures
below. It counts as hand-checked only once a person has gone through it.

**Setup.**
- The year is the business's first: 1 August 2025 (the start date) to 31 March 2026. The ledger only
  holds August and September, because that's all the statements cover, so these are the statements as
  they stand at 30 September, not a full year. It isn't 12 months, so it's headed "For the period
  ended 31 March 2026" ("Part years").
- There's no prior year, so there's no comparative column: the ReportDoc says `comparatives: none`, and
  every prior amount is blank, not nil ("Statement amounts and columns").
- The rounding priority list is the practice default: 290, 205, 245, 225, 120, 960, 810.
- Lines with no balance are left out ("Nil lines", still provisional): other income, every fixed asset
  line, accounts payable, credit cards, loans, opening equity, and money out not yet sorted (965 is nil
  once every line is coded). So are the fixed assets and non-current liabilities groups, whose lines
  are all left out.
