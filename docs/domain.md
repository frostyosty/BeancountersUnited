# Domain

The glossary and worked examples for acct. Accounting presentation decisions (signs, grouping, wording,
rounding placement) are recorded here once the practice has answered them. Nothing here is guessed. A
question that isn't answered yet is listed under "Open" with the milestone that needs it.

## Glossary

**Practice.** The accounting firm using acct. It owns the practice-level defaults: master charts, asset
classes, templates, mappings, the paragraph library and settings such as the GST rate.

**Client.** A business or entity the practice prepares accounts for. Each client has an entity type
(company first; others later).

**Client-year.** One client's financial year, with explicit start and end dates. It's the unit that holds
journals, gets finalised and produces statements. A first or last year can be shorter or longer than 12
months.

**Master chart.** The practice's chart of accounts for one entity type. A client's chart starts as a copy of
it and then belongs to the client.

**Chart / account.** A client's list of accounts. Each account has a code, a name, a type (asset,
liability, equity, income or expense) and an active flag.

**Account code.** An account's identifier, such as `200` or `200.01`. Codes sort by numeric segment, so
`200` < `200.01` < `1100`.

**Journal.** A dated set of at least two lines that sum to zero. Debits are positive and credits negative.
Posted journals never change.

**Reversing journal.** A journal that cancels an earlier one (`reverses_journal_id`). It's the only way to
correct a posted journal.

**System journal.** A journal the system generates, from TB import or depreciation. It's regenerated by
reversing the old one and posting a new one inside one command.

**Trial balance (TB).** Each account's balance at a date, computed from the opening balances plus the
year's journals.

**TB import.** Loading a client's trial balance from their own books. The TB-import journal is the
imported TB less the computed opening balances, so journals posted on top of it (adjustments) survive a
re-import.

**Books source.** Where a client-year's transactions come from: TB import or bank coding, never both.

**Rollover.** Computing a year's opening balances from the prior year. Balance-sheet accounts carry
forward. P&L accounts close to the client's designated equity account (retained earnings, for a company).
Rollover is computed, never posted.

**Template.** The layout of a set of statements for an entity type: a tree of report lines (groups, lines,
subtotals and totals), each with a stable key and a presentation sign.

**Report line.** One line in a template, identified by its key.

**Mapping.** A versioned set of account-code ranges, each pointing to a report line. Every account with a
non-zero balance must map to exactly one line.

**ReportDoc.** The built statements as data: sections, lines, current and prior amounts, styles and the
account codes behind each line. See ADR 003.

**Rounding line.** A designated report line that absorbs the differences left when individual lines are
rounded to whole dollars, so every column foots.

**Comparatives.** The prior year's figures shown beside the current year's. They come from the prior
year's snapshot TB presented through the current year's mapping and template.

**Finalise.** Lock a client-year once its statements are signed. It stores a snapshot and rejects any
later change to the year's numbers. Only a master user can reopen a year, and they must give a reason.

**Snapshot.** What finalising stores: the TB, the pinned mapping and template versions, the ReportDoc and
a SHA-256 of the ReportDoc JSON.

**Paragraph library.** The practice's own text for accounting policies and notes, with client fields
filled in.

**Asset register.** A client's fixed assets, with cost, acquisition date, depreciation settings and any
disposal. It drives depreciation journals and the fixed asset schedule.

**Asset class.** A practice-level grouping of assets with a default depreciation method, rate and
conventions, plus the accounts its journals post to. Clients can override these.

**Rate source.** Where an asset's depreciation settings came from: `practice`, `client` or `custom`.

**DV (diminishing value).** Depreciation at a rate on the book value at the start of the year (or on cost in
the year of acquisition).

**SL (straight line).** Depreciation at a rate on cost less residual value, stopping at the residual.

**Part-year convention.** How depreciation is prorated in the year an asset is acquired.

**Disposal-year convention.** How depreciation is treated in the year an asset is disposed of.

**GST-exclusive ledger.** A ledger whose coded bank lines are split into the net amount and GST.

## Decisions

### Rounding to whole dollars (decided 2026-09-23)
Statements show whole dollars. Rounding follows these rules, applied separately to the current and prior
columns:

1. **Lines.** Each line's exact cents total is rounded half away from zero.
2. **Totals.** Every total and subtotal shows its own exact cents total, rounded half away from zero. It
   is never just the sum of the rounded lines.
3. **Absorbing the difference.** Where the rounded lines in a section don't add up to that section's
   rounded total, the whole difference goes onto one line in that section, so the column foots. It's
   usually $1 but can be more when a section has many lines.
4. **Which line absorbs it.** Each client has a *rounding priority list*: an ordered list of account
   codes. For a section needing an adjustment, take the first account on the list that maps into that
   section and has a non-zero balance in that column. The line it maps to absorbs the difference.
   Example: the list is 1. Repairs & maintenance, 2. Bank fees. There's no R&M this year, and bank
   fees is $120, so bank fees shows $121.
5. **Fallback.** If no account on the list qualifies, the largest line in the section by absolute
   rounded amount absorbs it (ties go to the first in template order). The ReportDoc then carries a
   warning, so staff can extend the list.
6. **When totals conflict.** Rounding every total independently can leave totals that don't agree
   (income $100.40, expenses $50.60, profit $49.80: rounded 100 − 51 ≠ 50). Protected totals win, in
   this order: net profit, total assets, total equity (= net assets). A total that isn't protected
   (total income, total expenses, total liabilities, other subtotals) gives way. It shows whatever
   keeps the protected totals right, even if that's $1 off its own exact rounded figure. In the example,
   profit shows $50, and expenses show $50 via the absorbing line.
7. **Profit into equity.** The current-year profit shown in equity is the P&L's net profit exactly
   as shown.
8. **Drill-down.** Accounts behind a line always show their real balances. An absorbing line also shows
   a separate "rounding adjustment" entry, so the drill-down adds up to the line.
9. **Who sets the list.** The master user keeps a practice default list per entity type. Each new client
   gets a copy. Staff can reorder or edit a client's list. Changing the default doesn't alter existing
   clients unless someone runs the explicit "push default" command, which previews the change and only
   touches open years.

There's one implementation of this, in `acct-core`.

How it runs (`acct-core::reportdoc`), per column: lines start at their exact amount rounded;
then, statement by statement, links copy their already-settled target, groups (innermost first)
are brought to their exact total rounded, and totals are brought to theirs, protected ones first.
Once a protected row is settled, the lines under it are frozen, so nothing later can move it.
Protection is marked in the template (`protect: 1, 2, 3`), not hard-coded by name.

### Nil lines (provisional, to confirm)
A line that shows $0 in both columns is left out, along with any group whose lines are all left
out. Totals such as net profit always show. The accounts on a left-out line then don't appear in
drill-down, although they still count in the totals, so this needs the practice's confirmation.

### Account codes (decided 2026-09-23)
- A code is one or more segments separated by `.`, such as `200`, `200.01`, `1100` or `A100`. Segments
  are ASCII letters and digits, and codes are at most 20 characters. Letters are stored uppercase, so
  `a100` and `A100` are the same code.
- Codes sort segment by segment. An all-digit segment compares as a number, so `200` < `200.01` < `1100`,
  and a digit segment sorts before a segment containing letters. A code that is a prefix of another sorts
  first. `0200` and `200` are different codes; the shorter one sorts first.
- A mapping range `from`–`to` includes both ends, and `to` also covers its own sub-accounts: `200`–`299`
  includes `299.05`. Ranges may not overlap, and touching ends such as `100`–`199` and `199`–`250`
  count as overlapping.
- The master user builds master charts and templates. The practice ships premade charts in the familiar
  NZ style (similar code ranges and groupings to common small-business charts), written in our own
  words and not copied from any vendor (hard rule 3). Staff can reorganise a client's own chart.
